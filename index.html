Part one · Collecting the sample

Before you test

The sample matters more than the technique. Getting this part right is what makes the result reliable.

  1. Use a clean, dry container

    Collect the urine sample in a clean, dry container. Any residue or moisture in the container can affect the result.

  2. First urine of the morning is best

    hCG is most concentrated in your first sample of the day, which makes the test most sensitive at that time.

  3. Testing later in the day? Hold for 4 hours

    Avoid urinating for at least four hours before collecting the sample, and avoid drinking large amounts of fluid beforehand — dilute urine can produce a falsely negative result.

Timing your test

For the most reliable result, test on the day of your missed period or later. Testing earlier than this can produce a negative result even if you are pregnant.

Part two · Running the test

How to use the kit

Four steps, about a minute of handling, then a five-minute wait.

  1. Take out the device and lay it flat

    Remove the test device from the pouch just before use and place it on a clean, flat surface. Don't leave it out and exposed for long before testing.

  2. Find the three markings

    Every device is marked S, C and T. You'll only ever add urine to S.

  3. Add exactly 3 drops into S

    Draw up the sample with the dropper and add three drops into the S well. Do not add urine to C, to T, or to any other part of the device.

  4. Keep it away from moving air

    Fans, coolers and open windows can disturb the flow across the strip. Leave the device still and undisturbed while it develops.

The next step

The Beta hCG blood test

Your kit tells you whether the pregnancy hormone is present. A Beta hCG blood test measures how much of it there is — which is a different, and more precise, kind of answer.

What it is

Beta hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin) is a laboratory blood test that measures the level of the pregnancy hormone in your body, rather than simply detecting whether it is present.

Why it matters

  • Confirms pregnancy with laboratory-grade certainty
  • Detects pregnancy at an earlier stage than a urine test
  • Helps estimate the stage of pregnancy
  • Can indicate related abnormalities through serial monitoring

When to take it

  • After a positive result on your kit
  • When you want additional clinical confirmation
  • When your doctor recommends it

Read before you rely on the result

Things that change the answer

  • Read the result within five minutes. A result observed after five minutes should not be interpreted — evaporation lines can appear and it may not be accurate.
  • For best results, test on the day of your missed period or later.
  • If the result is negative but your period is still delayed, wait one week and repeat the test with a fresh device.
  • Drinking large amounts of fluid before collecting the sample can dilute hCG in the urine and lead to an inaccurate result.
  • Fertility medications containing hCG can interfere with the result. Tell your doctor if you are taking any.
  • Confirm your pregnancy with a doctor before beginning any endocrinology-related treatment. As with all diagnostic tests, a confirmed diagnosis should be made by a qualified gynaecologist.

Common questions

Still unsure?

The second line is very faint. Am I pregnant?

A faint line at T is still a positive result, as long as there's also a line at C. Line intensity depends on how much hCG is in your urine, which is usually lower very early in pregnancy or in a dilute sample. It does not indicate a weaker or less certain pregnancy. Confirm with a Beta hCG blood test.

Can I test at night instead of first thing in the morning?

Yes, but hold your urine for at least four hours beforehand and avoid drinking large amounts of fluid. Your first sample of the morning is the most concentrated and gives the most reliable result, especially in early pregnancy.

The test was negative but my period is still late.

Wait one week and repeat the test with a fresh device and a fresh sample. If you test too early, hCG levels may be too low to detect. If your period remains delayed after a second negative result, see a doctor.

No lines appeared, or a line appeared only at T. What now?

The test is invalid — it doesn't mean negative. The control line at C must appear for the result to be valid. Repeat the test using a fresh device and a fresh urine sample, following the instructions on this page.
